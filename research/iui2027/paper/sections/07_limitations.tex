\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

The evaluated environments form a small convenience corpus. Two are
exhibition-like spaces, and all are implemented with the same Unity and backend
stack. Results therefore demonstrate behavior in these cases rather than broad
generalization to arbitrary virtual environments.

Language-model responses can vary even when prompts and scenes are held
constant. We record model settings and repeated trials, but provider-side model
changes may remain outside our control. Structural grounding and semantic
response quality are reported separately because a correct route does not
guarantee a correct answer.

The direct-wiring baseline may favor developers familiar with the existing
implementation, while the model-grounded condition benefits from automated
validators created by the authors. We reduce this threat by scripting
reproducible change tasks, preserving complete patches, and reporting concrete
edited artifacts and failures rather than subjective effort estimates.

Fault injection establishes whether known inconsistency classes are detected;
it cannot prove the absence of unknown faults. Similarly, complete trace
coverage means that an evaluated runtime event is connected to model elements,
not that the model captures every aspect of the user's intent.
