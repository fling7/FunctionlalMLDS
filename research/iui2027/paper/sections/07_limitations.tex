\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

The three environments are a purposive development corpus; every natural object
has an asset-level owner, so group and zone fallback use synthetic copies.
Results establish behavior for these models, not population frequencies.

The comparison covers neither complete applications nor human authoring. Three
common mutations and scripted edits do not measure effort or unknown faults.
Both representations share semantic sources and one resolver, so parity is a
controlled consistency check, not independent routing evidence or architectural
superiority. The runtime also takes expected routes from the direct adapter and
checks binding fields from one pinned model; neither is a semantic oracle.

We evaluate neither answer quality nor usability, trust, workload, or
preference, and no held-out third-party scene tests generalization. The server
checks entity, route, and evidence but cannot prove that client-reported ray
geometry is physical; deployment needs a trusted client and transport boundary.
Unity evidence is limited to desktop selection and Editor batch paths, with no
headset, WebXR, or full-stack latency result.

The evaluated rooms are static during a session. A changed project is detected
against the pinned fingerprint, but there is no live model refresh, session
migration, or policy for newly created or merged objects. Different session
identifiers hold separate histories, yet simultaneous users and concurrent
writes to one session were not tested. Online spatial routing supports one
direct handoff per request, not transitive traversal within a turn. Authoring
covers placement, not responsibility, capability, or handoff editing.

The V2 assembler and validator share a codebase, so their agreement is not
independent standards conformance; full EAST-ADL conformance is not claimed.
Timing covers in-memory work on one Windows machine and excludes parsing,
generation, Unity, network, and model latency. Finally, frozen semantic inputs
came from model calls without a provider seed: stored responses are replayable,
but service generation is not bit-for-bit reproducible. Structural measurements
are deterministic and API-free; editorial feedback supplies no evaluation
evidence.
