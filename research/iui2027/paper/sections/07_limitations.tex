\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

The three environments are a purposive convenience corpus, two are
exhibition-like, and every natural-corpus object has an asset-level owner.
Group and zone fallback therefore use synthetic copied candidates. Results
establish behavior for these models, not population frequencies.

The comparison holds meaning constant but covers neither complete applications
nor human authoring. Its three common mutations and scripted edit counts do not
measure developer effort or unknown faults. Both representations derive from
the same semantic sources, and one resolver classifies both normalized graphs;
parity is a controlled representation check, not independent routing evidence
or proof of architectural superiority. Likewise, the runtime sweep takes route
and provider expectations from the direct adapter, while binding checks compare
fields derived from one pinned model. These are consistency checks, not
independent semantic oracles.

We evaluate neither answer quality nor usability, trust, workload, or
preference. The server checks entity, route, and evidence but cannot prove that
client-reported ray geometry is physical; deployment needs a trusted client and
transport boundary. Unity evidence is limited to desktop selection and Editor
batch paths, with no headset or WebXR result. Authoring covers placement, not
responsibility, capability, or handoff editing.

The V2 assembler and validator share a codebase, so their agreement is not
independent standards conformance; full EAST-ADL conformance is not claimed.
Timing covers in-memory work on one Windows machine and excludes parsing,
generation, Unity, network, and model latency. Finally, frozen semantic inputs
came from model calls without a provider seed: stored responses are replayable,
but service generation is not bit-for-bit reproducible. Structural measurements
are deterministic and API-free; editorial feedback supplies no evaluation
evidence.
