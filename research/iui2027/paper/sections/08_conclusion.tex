\section{Conclusion}

We presented an interaction-model-grounded assurance layer for embodied
multi-agent interfaces in Unity. Rather than replacing interaction design with
a metamodel, it requires each admitted request to follow one pinned chain from
spatial target through agent capability and runtime action, while a rejection
identifies the violated relation.

All 93 assets had complete provider-coherent chains, and all 186 declared
target-specific candidates were structurally complete. Under a shared resolver,
the adapters agreed on 93 owner and 459 route decisions and detected all nine
common injected faults. The runtime sweep admitted all 298 local or direct
routes with consistent binding evidence and rejected all 161 transitive or
unreachable routes before the response stub without session mutation. Unity
batch tests exercised 31 scenario contexts and 73 actions; four placement
workflows preserved preview and recovery guarantees.

These corpus-bounded results support \systemname{} as an executable assurance
boundary, not claims of better answers, usability, trust, or authoring
productivity. Whether the added structure helps people design or understand
spatial-agent interactions remains a human-participant question.
