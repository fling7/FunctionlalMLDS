\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

Study T is exhaustive for three purposively authored development cases, not for
a population of rooms or authors.  Every natural asset has an asset-level
owner, so group and zone fallback and ambiguity are tested only in derived
copies.  Both representations originate from shared semantic artifacts and
use one routing classifier after separate parsing; agreement therefore
establishes migration parity, not an independent validation of the policy.
The mutation catalog is small, the assembler and validators share a codebase,
and the deterministic response stub isolates contract behavior rather than
answer quality.  The timing benchmark covers only in-memory adapter work.

Study U is a single-condition convenience study of one brand room.  The current
cut contains 65 browser sessions across desktop/laptop and tablet/smartphone
devices, no headset sessions, and a young, technically weighted sample.  Its
approximately 12-percentage-point worst-case Wilson half-width supports broad
descriptive patterns, not fine subgroup or population claims.  Without a
baseline, the ratings cannot be causally attributed to FunctionalMLDS rather
than the implemented interface and tasks.

The two user tasks had a fixed order and exercised only successful grounding
and a permitted direct handoff.  They do not establish comprehension of
ambiguity, unavailable capabilities, unreachable providers, or rollback.
Error feedback was rated by a self-selected subset of 22 participants and is
exploratory.  The questionnaire records perceived, not backend-linked,
handoffs; its custom items are not a validated scale.  Long-term use, trust,
workload, immersion, and developer diagnosis remain outside the study.

The prototype pins a static model per session, permits one direct handoff per
request, and does not evaluate live migration, concurrent writes, or multiple
visitors.  The selected EAST-ADL subset provides traceability concepts rather
than independently assessed full conformance.  These boundaries narrow the
claims but do not change the observed corpus and participant results.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented an executable interaction contract that binds a selected spatial
entity to one responsible agent, declared capability, runtime action, and
evidence record within a pinned model version.  The client exposes unresolved
selection, while the backend re-resolves identity, responsibility, route, and
action before generation and rolls back response-dependent violations.

Across three authored environments, all 93 assets and 186 target-specific
declaration pairs were complete.  Migration preserved all 93 owner decisions
and 459 route classifications under the shared policy; runtime enforcement
admitted all 298 local or direct routes and rejected all 161 transitive or
unreachable routes without retained mutation.  The strict provider contract
also detected all nine injected cross-layer faults based on relations absent
from the direct artifacts.

The user study complements this technical evidence.  Successful target--answer
and handoff interactions were generally understandable, but the responsibility
rule and value of several specialized agents were less consistently clear.
FunctionalMLDS therefore provides the evaluated fail-closed control and
evidence boundary; the next interface challenge is to communicate provider
rationale and blocked states as clearly as the system enforces them.
