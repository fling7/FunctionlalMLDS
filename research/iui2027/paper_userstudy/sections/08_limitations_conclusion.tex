\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

The technical evaluation establishes behavior for three purposively authored
development cases.  It does not estimate frequencies in a population of rooms,
domains, or authors.  Every natural asset has an explicit asset-level owner, so
group and zone fallback and ambiguity are exercised only in derived copies.  No
independently authored or held-out scene tests generalization.

The non-regression comparison is controlled rather than independent.  Both
representations originate from shared semantic artifacts and are classified by
one routing function after separate parsing.  The direct-wiring projection also
supplies expected owner and route classes for the runtime sweep.  Agreement
therefore detects migration and serialization divergence but cannot validate
the shared policy against an external oracle.  Binding identifiers are checked
for cross-field agreement within one pinned model, not against independently
authored labels.

Fault injection is systematic but small.  The common denominator contains
three mutation types, and the contract-only denominator contains three
additional types.  Detection and localization do not imply complete fault
coverage or automatic repair.  Scripted artifact and reference deltas are not
developer effort.  The V2 assembler, validators, and materializer also share a
codebase, so their agreement is not independent standards conformance.

Runtime tests replace the online response model with a deterministic structured
stub.  This isolates admission, evidence, call counts, and rollback, but does
not measure factual correctness, naturalness, refusal quality, or interaction
between generated content and the contract.  The local timing benchmark covers
only in-memory adapter work and excludes parsing, generation, Unity, network,
and model latency.

The user study adds human-centered evidence but has a different boundary.  It is
an unmoderated, single-condition convenience study of one implemented brand
room, with 45 desktop/laptop and 20 tablet/smartphone sessions and no headset
response in the current data cut.  Device heterogeneity, self-selection, and the
young, technically weighted sample limit generalization.  With 65 sessions,
binary proportions have at worst approximately a 12-percentage-point 95\%
Wilson half-width; this supports broad descriptive patterns, not fine subgroup
comparisons or population estimates.  Without a baseline, the study cannot
attribute usability or comprehension to FunctionalMLDS rather than the
particular interface and tasks.

The questionnaire records perceived handoffs but not linked per-session backend
handoff events.  It therefore cannot test individual log--report agreement.
Error feedback was rated by only 22 participants who happened to experience a
relevant state, and that result remains exploratory.  The custom items are not
a validated psychometric scale, and the study does not evaluate long-term use,
trust calibration, workload, immersion, or developer diagnosis.  It tests
whether the current cues support target, answer, handoff, and responsibility
comprehension.

The evaluated project remains static within a session.  Drift is detected, but
there is no live migration for objects created, deleted, merged, or rebound
during a conversation.  Online routing permits at most one direct handoff per
request; transitive reachability is an offline diagnostic.  Concurrent writes
to one session and coordination among simultaneous visitors were not tested.
Finally, the selected EAST-ADL subset serves as a traceability foundation; full
EAST-ADL conformance has not been independently assessed.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented an executable interaction contract that keeps a selected spatial
entity, responsible embodied agent, declared capability, concrete backend
action, and returned evidence within one pinned model version.  The client
makes unresolved selection explicit, while the backend re-resolves identity,
responsibility, route, and action before generation and rolls back
response-dependent violations.

Across three authored environments, all 93 assets and 186 target-specific
declaration pairs were complete.  Migration preserved all 93 owner decisions
and 459 route classifications under a shared policy.  The runtime admitted all
298 local or direct routes with matching evidence and rejected all 161
transitive or unreachable routes before the response stub without retained
mutation.  A strict provider contract additionally detected and localized all
nine injected cross-layer faults that rely on relations absent from the direct
artifacts.

The complementary user study shows why technical enforcement and interface
intelligibility must be evaluated separately.  Participants generally reported
clear target--answer fit and visible handoffs, but only 37/65 identified the
intended object-and-topic responsibility rule, and the usefulness of several
specialized agents received mixed ratings.  FunctionalMLDS can therefore
provide the evaluated fail-closed control and evidence boundary, while the
current interface still needs clearer responsibility communication.  The
results are initial and descriptive; they do not establish better dialogue or
causal usability improvement over an alternative interface.
