\section{Evaluation Method}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Complementary two-study design}

We evaluate the same contract at two different boundaries.  \emph{Study T}
addresses RQ1--RQ3 with deterministic software evidence: whether the authored
relations are complete, survive migration, reject invalid requests, and add
enforceable invariants at measurable structural cost.  \emph{Study U} addresses
RQ4 with a remote, unmoderated, task-based user evaluation: whether the
implemented target, provider, and handoff cues are understandable in use.  The
studies are complementary rather than interchangeable.  Study T cannot
establish human comprehension, while Study U cannot validate the internal
contract or show superiority over another architecture.

\subsection{Study T: contract coverage and enforcement}

The technical corpus comprises the three authored development cases in the
repository: a career-fair room, a classroom, and the food/trade-fair brand room
(93 routable assets and 15 agents in total).  A fresh native V2 instance is
regenerated from each frozen version-0.5 case.  An asset is complete only when
each asset-targeting \texttt{ScenarioStep}/\texttt{CapabilityUse} pair resolves
one coherent provider, capability, binding, action, assertion set, and covering
validation case.

Migration non-regression is checked with separately parsed direct-wiring and V2
adapters.  Both expose ownership candidates and handoff edges to one fixed
policy: asset ownership precedes group and zone ownership, and several owners at
the highest active tier are ambiguous.  We compare the owner of every asset and
the route from every modeled starting agent.  Because one shared function
classifies the normalized views, agreement tests representation parity, not the
correctness of the policy.  Derived copies separately exercise group fallback,
zone fallback, and ambiguity because every natural corpus asset has an explicit
asset owner.

Runtime enforcement converts all 459 object--start-agent pairs into backend
requests.  Local and direct routes must preserve target, provider, capability,
binding, and action evidence; transitive and unreachable routes must stop
before the deterministic response stub without retained state mutation.  A
targeted suite adds one valid and 16 invalid spatial requests, and three Unity
Editor smokes execute the generated client bridge and evidence checks.  Common
mutations remove ownership, add a competing owner, or dangle a handoff.
Contract-only mutations remove a capability-use provider, duplicate a runtime
agent identifier, or assign domain behavior to the orchestrator.  We record
detection, localization, false positives, representation edits, and an
alternating 40-repetition in-memory timing benchmark.  All technical runs are
API-free; timing excludes file I/O, generation, Unity, networking, and model
latency.

\subsection{Study U: browser-based interaction study}

Study U evaluates the user-visible realization, not the metamodel in the
abstract.  Participants used the browser-based 3D brand room and completed two
standardized tasks while a short questionnaire guided the session.  First, they
selected a product exhibit and asked the Product Specialist a grounded question,
exercising target visibility and answer--target fit.  Second, they asked the
Reception Agent a product question designed to invoke the declared direct
handoff to the Product Specialist, exercising handoff awareness and final-agent
clarity.  Immediate post-task questions reduce recall demands; a final block
measures perceived responsibility, predictability, guidance, ease, and the
usefulness of specialized agents.

The instrument uses five-point agreement items, a 0--10 overall score, one
multiple-choice comprehension check, device and experience questions, and
optional formative comments.  We treat the comprehension proportion as the
primary human-study outcome; the custom ratings are secondary.  The
comprehension check asks whether provider selection depends on the object and
question topic; its distractors encode nearest-agent, first-contacted-agent, and
unclear mental models.  The introduction describes the multi-agent setting
without disclosing this rule.  There is no comparison condition, so Study U
supplies descriptive evidence about the implemented cues and cannot establish
that FunctionalMLDS improves usability over direct wiring or another interface.

\subsection{Participants, sample adequacy, and analysis}

The analysis cut of 17 August 2026 contains 65 unique completed sessions:
45 on desktop or laptop browsers and 20 on tablets or smartphones.  Participants
were 18--54 years old (41 aged 18--24, 20 aged 25--34, and 4 aged 45--54);
44 reported computing, engineering, or design/HCI backgrounds, 11 business,
and 10 other fields.  This convenience sample characterizes the current
prototype audience and is not population-representative.

Because Study U is a single-condition descriptive evaluation, an effect-size
power calculation for a group difference would be inappropriate.  We instead
judge adequacy by estimation precision.  With \(n=65\), the largest two-sided
95\% Wilson interval half-width for a binary proportion is approximately
0.12 at \(p=.5\) \cite{wilson1927probable}.  This is sufficient for the intended
broad item-level and majority-versus-ambiguity claims, but not for small
subgroup differences or population estimates.  Later responses will be analyzed
as a new dated data cut rather than silently appended to the present results.

For Study T we report exact corpus counts and descriptive timing; confidence
intervals would imply a sampling model the exhaustive probes do not have.  For
Study U we report item medians and interquartile ranges, the proportion rating
an item 4 or 5, and categorical counts; the comprehension proportion receives a
95\% Wilson interval.  Handoff ratings are summarized among participants who
reported observing a handoff, and error-feedback ratings only among those who
experienced such a state.  We do not combine the custom items into an
unvalidated omnibus scale or perform confirmatory subgroup tests.  Optional
comments are used formatively rather than treated as a saturated qualitative
study.

The questionnaire export records perceived handoffs but no per-session backend
handoff event.  Consequently, RQ4 concerns whether participants reported and
understood the transition; Study T independently establishes that the modeled
handoff path is executable.  The dated questionnaire export, analysis code,
technical inputs, mutations, and raw probe outcomes form the reproducibility
boundary.
