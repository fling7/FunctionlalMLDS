\begin{abstract}
People increasingly interact with multiple embodied agents in immersive 3D
environments, where agents have distinct responsibilities and capabilities.
Such interfaces require more than fluent dialogue: the selected object,
responsible agent, permitted capability, concrete runtime action, and returned
evidence must remain consistent across the spatial client and agent backend.
We present \systemname{}, an executable interaction-contract approach that
represents these relations in a machine-readable, traceable model and
materializes coordinated artifacts for both layers.  At runtime, a pinned
contract is authoritative.  The backend re-resolves the selected entity,
applies asset-, group-, and zone-level responsibility, verifies at most one
declared handoff, selects one target- and provider-specific capability binding,
and checks returned evidence before presentation.  Request-decidable violations
stop before response generation and retained state mutation; response-dependent
violations trigger rollback with relation-level diagnostics.

We evaluate the approach through two complementary studies.  Study T performs
deterministic model analysis, systematic fault injection, and end-to-end
contract tests across three authored environments.  All 93 assets and 186
target-specific declaration pairs were complete.  Direct-wiring and contract
adapters agreed on all 93 owner decisions and 459 route classifications under a
shared policy.  Runtime tests admitted all 298 local or direct routes and
rejected all 161 transitive or unreachable routes without retained mutation;
the strict provider contract also detected and localized all nine injected
contract-only faults.  Study U is a remote, unmoderated, task-based evaluation
with 65 completed browser sessions.  Fifty participants rated answer--target
fit positively and 55 found the interface guidance helpful.  Of 49 who reported
observing the intended handoff, 40 found it understandable and 45 found the
final responding agent clear.  However, only 37 participants identified the
intended object-and-topic responsibility rule.  \systemname{} therefore
provides a fail-closed control and evidence boundary with initial evidence that
successful target and handoff cues are intelligible; clearer responsibility and
failure communication remains necessary.
\end{abstract}
